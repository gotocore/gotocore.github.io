1. What privacy means

Privacy, as used in this policy, refers to the practices Core Products & Services, LLC does not undertake. There are no third-party ads, no tracking or analytics, no profiles, no cloud, no Core servers, no social features, and no AI. Core does not encrypt files except the Gallery vault. Core does not operate an anonymizing service. When Core Weather obtains a place through Detect, coordinates are rounded to approximately 0.01° before the forecast request is sent.

2. Core Weather

Core Weather is provided free of charge. It shows Dual °F/°C for a place entered by the user, or for the device location only if Detect is chosen. Detect is one-shot and opt-in. It is not continuous tracking, and it is not saved as a profile. Core Weather requires a connection for the forecast, place search, and alerts.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings (temperature units, sound, vibration, alert options, theme).
  • Your saved place (the text you typed, or the place that Detect location found).
  • The last forecast, with the rounded coordinates for your saved place and a list of alerts already shown, so widgets and notifications don't repeat themselves.
  • This data is kept in the app's private storage. Android backup is turned off for Core Weather, so this data is not copied to your Google backup.

What leaves your device

Core Weather needs the internet. All requests use HTTPS. Every request goes straight from your phone to the service, so that service sees your IP address. Requests also carry a User-Agent label that names Core Weather.

  • Place lookup (when you type a place): the city or postal code you type is sent as you typed it. We cannot round a typed place name. Services used: Zippopotam.us (5-digit U.S. ZIP codes), Open-Meteo Geocoding, Nominatim (OpenStreetMap), and the U.S. Census Geocoder (backup for ZIP codes only).
  • Detect location (optional): the app asks Android for your location one time, only when you tap Detect location. Before any network request, it rounds the coordinates to 2 decimal places (about 0.01°, roughly 0.5–1.1 km). The rounded point goes to Nominatim (OpenStreetMap) to find the place name, with BigDataCloud as a backup. Then Detect location turns itself off. The app keeps the place it found, not a live location.
  • Forecasts: rounded coordinates go to Open-Meteo.
  • U.S. observations and severe alerts: for U.S. places, rounded coordinates also go to the U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov). Outside the U.S., NWS is not contacted.
  • Background refresh: while you have a saved place, Core Weather refreshes on a schedule (about every 15 minutes or longer, only when you have a network connection). It also schedules a refresh after your phone restarts. Each refresh sends your saved place to the services above again. Background refresh never turns on GPS.
  • Links you tap: "More on this warning" and similar links open forecast.weather.gov or Windy.com in your browser, with rounded coordinates or your ZIP code in the address. That website's own policy applies.

Third parties

Open-Meteo, Nominatim / OpenStreetMap, Zippopotam.us, U.S. Census Geocoder, BigDataCloud, U.S. National Weather Service, plus forecast.weather.gov or Windy.com only when you tap a link. Core has no control over their logs.

Permissions

  • Internet: to get forecasts and place data.
  • Location (approximate and precise): optional. Used only when you tap Detect location. Typing a city or ZIP works without it.
  • Notifications, vibration, full-screen alerts: for weather updates and severe-weather warnings. You can turn these off in the app or in Android settings.
  • Run at startup, wake lock: to keep the scheduled refresh running after a restart.

Purchases

None. Core Weather is free and has no in-app purchases.

3. Core Music

Core Music is a mobile-first music library manager and HiFi player. Playback uses the files on your device. It does not stream, and it has no cloud library. No account is required. A network connection is not required.*

What stays on your device

  • Your library index (track list, tags, folders), playlists, ratings, hearts, play settings, and EQ, kept in the app's private storage.
  • Album art the app saves for your library.
  • A small activity and crash log, kept in the app's cache. It stays on the phone unless you send it (see below).
  • Changes you make on purpose, like editing tags, saving cover art, or saving playlists, are written to your own music files or folders. Core Music also saves a ratings backup playlist (`CoreMusic-Ratings.m3u8`) about once a day, in your playlist folder or in app storage. It adds `.nomedia` files so its artwork does not show up in gallery apps.

Android backup

Android backup is turned on for Core Music. Android Auto Backup can include Music settings, EQ, the library index, ratings, hearts, and cached art and waveforms. It does not include your music files. Google runs that backup, not Core. You can turn it off in Android's backup settings.

What leaves your device

*A connection is optional, and only when you ask for missing artwork (iTunes, Qobuz, or Deezer) or missing lyrics (LRCLIB). Library and playback work offline. Core Music has no crash-reporting SDK and no update check. The crash-log email and the privacy-policy link open your email app and your browser. Nothing is sent until you send it.

Those lookups work like this:

  • Lyrics lookup: the track title, artist, and album (or the text you type) go to LRCLib (lrclib.net).
  • Album-art search: your search words go to iTunes Search (Apple), Qobuz, and/or Deezer. The artwork you pick is then downloaded from that service's image servers.

These services see your IP address and the search words needed to find a match. Browsing and playing your library does not need these lookups.

  • Sending a crash log (optional): tapping "Send Crash Log" opens Android's share menu with the log file attached and an email address filled in. Nothing is sent unless you finish sending it in the app you choose, such as your email app. The log can include app activity, error details, and timestamps.
  • Your phone's media controls: like other music players, Core Music shares the current track's title, artist, and artwork with Android's media controls, the lock screen, and any connected devices you use, such as Bluetooth or Android Auto.

Third parties

LRCLib, Apple (iTunes Search), Qobuz, and Deezer, only when you start a lookup. Google Play handles purchases (see below).

Permissions

  • Music and video files / storage, and folders you pick: to find and play your files. Core Music can only reach the folders you choose with Android's folder picker.
  • Internet and network state: only for the optional lookups.
  • Notifications and foreground services: for playback controls and library scanning.
  • Battery optimization exemption (asked, optional): so playback and scans aren't stopped.
  • USB host (optional feature): for USB audio devices (DACs).

Purchases

Core Music offers a 14-day free trial, then a one-time $4.99 unlock through Google Play. Google calls this kind of purchase a non-consumable one-time product: it is a single charge, it does not renew, and once bought it stays associated with the Google Account that bought it. Google Play processes the payment and decides refunds. Core does not receive your card details, and Core does not issue a separate license you can hand to someone else. Google’s own terms allow a transfer between Google Accounts only when you own both accounts and Google has turned that transfer on for Play. Signing in on another phone with the same Google Account is how the unlock is recognized again.

4. Core Gallery

Core Gallery is a paid app ($2.99, no trial) for viewing and organizing photos and videos on the device. Its vault is encrypted. The rest of the app is not. It has no accounts and no network connection at all.

No internet

Core Gallery does not request Android's internet permission, so the app cannot connect to the internet. Your photos are not uploaded anywhere by Core Gallery, and editing and enhancing happen on the phone.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings: albums, groups, pins, hidden albums, sort and filter options, local favorites and tags, widget options, and app lock.
  • Thumbnails and an image cache in the app's cache.
  • The apps you choose for "open with" or editing, so it can open them again next time.
  • Changes you choose to make, like edits, rotation, info/EXIF edits, and deletes, are saved to your own photo and video files through Android.
  • Android backup is turned off for Core Gallery.

Vault

You can move photos and videos into Core Gallery's Vault. The vault is encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key stays in the Android keystore and is wrapped by a PIN or a 9-dot pattern you set. The vault unlocks with that PIN or pattern only. Files in the vault are not in the normal photo library and do not show in the Files app. Leaving the vault locks it. Moving a picture out returns it to the album it came from.

  • Vault files are not backed up. If you uninstall Core Gallery or clear its data, Vault files are permanently deleted. Move anything you want to keep back out of the Vault first.

Photo location

  • Core Gallery does not use your phone's GPS and does not request location permission.
  • It asks for Android's media location permission so it can read location data already saved inside your photos (EXIF), for example to show or edit a photo's info.
  • An optional setting, Strip location when sharing, removes location data from photos before you share them. It is off by default and works on photos, not videos.

What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself. When you share, set a wallpaper, or open a photo or video in another app, Android sends that file to the app you pick. That app's own privacy policy then applies.

Third parties

None are contacted by Core Gallery. Google Play handles the purchase.

Permissions

  • Photos and videos (all or selected): to show your library.
  • Media location: to read location data stored in your photos (see above).
  • Set wallpaper: only when you choose to set one.

Purchases

Core Gallery is $2.99 with no trial. App purchases are completed or facilitated through the Google Play store. Google Play processes the payment and decides refunds. The purchase stays associated with the Google Account that bought it. Core does not receive card details, and Core does not issue a separate license transferable to another person. The app has no in-app purchases.

5. Core Workout

Core Workout is a paid exercise interval timer for Android. It runs timers on the device for formats such as EMOM, AMRAP, Interval, Tabata, For Time, HIIT, Custom, Favorites, and chained workouts. No account is required. Voice cues and workouts stay on the phone. Timing a workout does not require a network connection.

Network

Core Workout requests internet access only so Google Play Billing can complete or refresh a one-time unlock. Workouts, cues, and timers are not uploaded. After an unlock is stored on the device, you can time workouts offline. Core Workout has no crash-reporting SDK and no analytics.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings (volume, voice, vibration, keep-screen-on, and related options).
  • Saved workouts, presets you hide or copy, unfinished custom starts kept for later save, and chained workouts.
  • Built-in voice cue files that ship inside the app.
  • A local record of whether the one-time unlock is active.
  • This data is kept in the app's private storage. Android backup is turned on for Core Workout. Android Auto Backup can include settings and saved workouts. Google runs that backup, not Core. You can turn it off in Android's backup settings.

Backup and restore

Optional Backup and Restore use Android's system save and open sheets. Backup writes a CoreWorkout-backup.json file you choose a location for. Restore reads a file you pick and merges saved workouts by id. Core does not upload that file. A file that is not a Core Workout backup changes nothing.

What leaves your device

Opening the privacy-policy link opens your browser. A backup file you save can leave the phone if you copy or share that file yourself. Purchase and license checks go through Google Play when a network is available. Core Workout does not send workouts or voice cues to Core.

Third parties

Google Play Billing handles the one-time purchase and related license checks. Core does not contact other third parties from Core Workout.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: only for Google Play Billing unlock and license refresh.
  • Notifications and foreground service: so the timer can keep running with the screen off and show pause, resume, skip, and stop controls.
  • Vibration: optional cues you can turn off.
  • Wake lock: to keep the timer deadline accurate while the screen is off.
  • Battery optimization exemption (asked, optional): so the system is less likely to stop a running timer. The app opens the system settings list; you decide.

Purchases

Core Workout is a $5.99 one-time unlock through Google Play, with no trial. App purchases are completed or facilitated through the Google Play Store. Google Play processes the payment and decides refunds. The purchase stays associated with the Google Account that bought it. Core does not receive card details, and Core does not issue a separate license transferable to another person.

6. Children

Core apps are general-audience utilities. They are not made for children, and they have no accounts, profiles, chat, or social features. Core does not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you think a child has sent us personal information, for example in an email, contact us and we will delete it.

7. Your choices

  • Weather: type a place instead of using Detect location, turn off location permission, or turn off alerts and notifications.
  • Music: do not use the lyrics or album-art lookups, do not send the crash log, or turn off Android backup.
  • Gallery: turn on "Strip location when sharing," and use the app lock.
  • Workout: turn off vibration, lower or mute cue volume, or skip the optional battery-optimization exemption. Use Backup only if you want a file copy of saved workouts.
  • All apps: uninstalling an app or clearing its storage removes the data it keeps on your phone. Changes you saved to your own files stay. For Gallery, Vault contents are deleted.
  • Questions or requests: email gotocorepstx@gmail.com. Core keeps nothing about you on its own servers, so most questions are about what is stored on your phone.
