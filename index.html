Applies to: Core Gallery, Core Music, Core Notes, Core Scan, Core Sign, Core Utilities, Core Weather, Core Workout, and Trio Live Wallpapers for Android.

1. What privacy means

Privacy, as used in this policy, refers to the practices Core Products & Services, LLC does not undertake. Beyond the promises above, there are no analytics, no profiles, no cloud, no Core servers, and no social features. Core does not operate an anonymizing service. Encryption is used only where a section below says so: the Core Gallery vault, the Core Sign vault, everything Core Notes stores, and the FTP connections you save in Core Utilities. Core Weather, Core Music, Core Utilities, and Google Play purchases are the only things that use the internet, each described in its own section.

2. Purchases in all Core apps

Every Core purchase is completed or facilitated through Google Play. Google Play processes the payment and decides refunds. Core does not receive your card details, and Core does not issue a separate license you can hand to another person. Prices are in U.S. dollars and are shown on Google Play before you buy.

3. Rate or share pop-up

Core Gallery, Core Notes, Core Scan, Core Sign, Core Weather, Core Workout, and Trio Live Wallpapers can show a "Rate or share" pop-up now and then. It appears no sooner than 90 days after you install the app, and only after you have opened the app on at least 5 different days. After that, it appears at most once every 90 days. Core Scan also waits until you have saved a scan, Core Sign until you have exported a document, and Core Workout until you have finished a workout.

  • What stays on your device: to decide when to show the pop-up, the app keeps a few dates and counts (such as when it last showed the pop-up, the days you opened the app, and, where it applies, how many workouts you have finished) in its private storage. They are never sent anywhere and are not copied to your Google backup.
  • Rate on Google Play: opens the app's page in Android's Google Play Store app, or in your browser if the Play Store app can't open it. A rating or review you choose to leave there is handled by Google Play under Google's policies.
  • Share: opens Android's share menu with a short message and the app's Google Play link. Nothing is sent unless you pick an app and send it yourself, and that app's own policy then applies.
  • Not now: closes the pop-up, as do Back and tapping outside it.

The app itself sends nothing. The pop-up is built into each app and does not use Google Play's in-app review service.

4. Children

Core apps are general-audience utilities. They are not made for children, and they have no accounts, profiles, chat, or social features. Core does not knowingly collect personal information from children. Core Utilities is meant for ages 16 and up because of the M-Lab speed test. If you think a child has sent us personal information, for example in an email, contact us and we will delete it.

5. Your choices

  • Gallery: turn on "Strip location when sharing," use the app lock, and send the crash log only if you choose.
  • Music: leave the Artwork lookup and Lyrics lookup switches off, do not send the crash log, or turn off Android backup.
  • Notes: use the app lock, skip calendar import or deny calendar permission (.ics import still works), share a crash report only if you choose, leave Show alarms over other apps off (the default) or revoke Display over other apps in Android settings at any time, and keep your backup password or recovery code somewhere safe.
  • Scan: leave text recognition off if you don't need searchable PDFs. Saved scans in Documents › Core Scanned Documents are not encrypted, so delete the ones you no longer need. You can also send sensitive documents to Core Sign and keep them in its Vault (a Core Sign paid feature).
  • Sign: keep signatures and sensitive documents in the Vault, remember that exported files are not encrypted, and choose Copy rather than Link when you don't want Core Sign to be able to change or delete the original.
  • Utilities: leave Network tools off, use SFTP rather than plain FTP for File Share and the FTP client, and stop Wi-Fi File Share when you are done.
  • Weather: type a place instead of using Detect location, turn off location permission, or turn off alerts and notifications.
  • Workout: turn off vibration, lower or mute cue volume, or skip the optional battery-optimization exemption. Use Backup only if you want a file copy of saved workouts.
  • Trio Live Wallpapers: use Hidden Mode to keep saved wallpapers out of other apps, and share the crash log only if you choose.
  • All apps: uninstalling an app or clearing its storage removes the data it keeps on your device. Changes you saved to your own files stay. For Gallery and Sign, Vault contents are deleted; for Notes, all notes are deleted unless you made a backup. For Music and Workout, the trial start date can remain in Android backup and Google Play services Block Store (see each app's section).
  • Questions or requests: email gotocorepstx@gmail.com. Core keeps nothing about you on its own servers, so most questions are about what is stored on your device.

6. Changes to this policy

If an application's behavior changes, this policy and the effective date at the top will be updated. This page should be reviewed periodically. Material changes will also be noted in the relevant application's release notes.

8. Core Gallery

Core Gallery is a paid app ($2.99, no trial) for viewing and organizing photos and videos on the device. Its vault is encrypted. The rest of the app is not. It has no accounts.

No internet

Core Gallery does not request Android's internet permission, so the app cannot connect to the internet. Your photos are not uploaded anywhere by Core Gallery, and editing and enhancing happen on the device. The first time you open Core Gallery, Android's Google Play Store app checks your Google Play purchase, so that first start needs a connection.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings: albums (a new album you create is kept only as a name here until you first move or copy something into it), groups, pins, hidden albums, sort and filter options, local favorites and tags, widget options, and app lock.
  • Thumbnails of your photos and videos, kept in Core Gallery's private files folder and marked so other apps, galleries, and photo pickers don't list them, plus an image cache in the app's cache.
  • A list of your library, so Core Gallery opens quickly. Vault items are never in this list or in the thumbnails.
  • SVG thumbnails in the grid are rendered on the device and cached in Core Gallery's private app storage. They are never uploaded.
  • Startup and scroll timing, kept only in Core Gallery's on-device diagnostic log. It is never sent anywhere.
  • Small preview copies of the photos shown in your home-screen widgets, kept in the app's private files folder and marked so other apps' galleries don't list them. They are deleted when you remove the widget.
  • The apps you choose for "open with" or editing, so it can open them again next time.
  • A crash log, kept in the app's private storage (the last 5 crashes). It holds the error trace, the app version, the device model, the Android version, and memory numbers: how much memory Core Gallery was using and its limit, counts of its running tasks, of the photos in its library and in its saved and search lists, whether an accessibility service was on, the size and file type of the largest picture being opened, and counts of thumbnails that couldn't be made, by file type. It never includes file names, paths, photo data, or location.
  • After a crash, the next start skips restoring the last screen and offers to share the crash log. Nothing is sent automatically.
  • Changes you choose to make, like edits, rotation, info/EXIF edits, and deletes, are saved to your own photo and video files through Android.
  • When photos or videos are added to or removed from your device, Core Gallery can update its library and make new thumbnails in the background. This happens on the device, and nothing is sent.
  • Android backup is turned off for Core Gallery. Thumbnails, widget previews, the library list, and the Vault are also left out when Android moves app data to a new device.

Vault

You can move photos and videos into Core Gallery's Vault. The vault is encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key stays in the Android keystore and is wrapped by a PIN or a 9-dot pattern you set, or by a strong biometric if you turn it on. Biometric unlock is optional: Android checks it on the device, and Core never sees or stores your biometric data. Your PIN or pattern always works too. Files in the vault are not in the normal photo library and do not show in the Files app. Leaving the vault locks it. Moving a picture out returns it to the album it came from. Vault videos can't be opened in other apps, to keep them private.

  • Vault files are not backed up. If you uninstall Core Gallery or clear its data, Vault files are permanently deleted. Move anything you want to keep back out of the Vault first.

Photo location

  • Core Gallery does not use your device's GPS and does not request location permission.
  • It asks for Android's media location permission so it can read location data already saved inside your photos (EXIF), for example to show or edit a photo's info.
  • An optional setting, Strip location when sharing, removes location data from photos before you share them. It is off by default and works on photos, not videos.
  • Shrink video (optional, offered when you share or select videos) makes a smaller copy on the device, saved to Movies/Core Gallery. The copy never carries location data, whatever the Strip location setting is. Your original video is only read, never changed.

What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself, plus one check: when you first open Core Gallery, Android's Google Play Store app confirms your purchase with Google Play. Core Gallery sends nothing itself; Google's privacy policy applies to that check. The crash log stays on your device unless you choose to send it: Settings › About › Crash log opens Android's share menu with our email address filled in, and nothing is sent unless you finish sending it. When you share, set a wallpaper, or open a photo or video in another app, Android sends that file to the app you pick. That app's own privacy policy then applies.

  • Vectorize (images only): sends the picture you chose to Core Utilities on the same device, through Android, so it can be traced there. The first time, Core Gallery explains this before anything is sent. If Core Utilities is not installed, Core Gallery opens its Google Play page instead.
  • Choosing a photo for another app: when another app asks you to pick a photo, Core Gallery returns only the photo you choose to that app.

Third parties

Core Gallery contacts no third parties itself. Google Play handles the purchase, and Android's Google Play Store app checks it with Google Play when you first open the app.

Permissions

  • Photos and videos (all or selected): to show your library. The Downloads album shows only what is in your device's main Download folder.
  • Media location: to read location data stored in your photos (see above).
  • Set wallpaper: only when you choose to set one.
  • Biometric (optional): only if you turn on biometric unlock for the Vault.
  • Google Play license check: so Android's Google Play Store app can confirm your purchase.
  • Folder access (optional): if you tap "Allow Gallery to read this folder" in Zips, Android's folder picker lets Core Gallery read the zip files in the folder you choose.
  • Media management (optional): if you allow it in Android settings, Core Gallery can move, trash, or delete your photos and videos without asking each time. It only acts on files you choose in the app.
  • Run at startup, wake lock, and a short background task: added by Android's task scheduler so on-device work (such as widget updates and thumbnails for new photos and videos) can finish. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Purchases

Core Gallery is $2.99 with no trial, paid on Google Play. The app has no in-app purchases.

9. Core Music

Core Music is a mobile-first music library manager and HiFi player. Playback uses the files on your device. It does not stream, and it has no cloud library. No account is required. A network connection is not required.*

What stays on your device

  • Your library index (track list, tags, folders), playlists, ratings, hearts, play settings, and EQ, kept in the app's private storage.
  • Album art the app saves for your library.
  • A small activity log, kept in the app's cache. It stays on the device unless you send it (see below).
  • A crash log of the last 5 crashes, kept in the app's private storage: the error trace, the app version, the device model, and the Android version. File paths, URIs, coordinates, and media file names are removed. It stays on the device unless you choose to share it.
  • Changes you make on purpose, like editing tags, saving cover art, or saving playlists, are written to your own music files or folders. Core Music also saves a ratings backup playlist (CoreMusic-Ratings.m3u8) about once a day, in your playlist folder or in app storage. It adds .nomedia files so its artwork does not show up in gallery apps.

Android backup

Android backup is turned on for Core Music. Android Auto Backup can include Music settings, EQ, the library index, ratings, hearts, play counts, the Network Features switches, your trial start date, and your unlock status. It does not include your music files or cached art and waveforms. Google runs that backup, not Core. You can turn it off in Android's backup settings.

Core Music saves the date your 14-day trial started in the app's private storage on this device. Android backup and Google Play services Block Store keep a copy for you. Block Store copies it to your Google backup only when Google can end-to-end encrypt it. That way, clearing data or reinstalling usually doesn't restart the trial. Google runs these, not Core; Core never receives the date.

What leaves your device

*A connection is optional. Core Music has two Network Features switches in Settings, Artwork lookup and Lyrics lookup. Both start off, and nothing is looked up until you turn one on. Library and playback work offline. Core Music has no crash-reporting SDK and no update check. Google Play's billing library may send its own diagnostic data to Google. Core receives none of it.

Those lookups work like this:

  • Lyrics lookup (off until you turn it on): the song title, artist, album, and length (or the text you type) go to LRCLIB (lrclib.net). You choose the lyrics before they are saved.
  • Artwork lookup (off until you turn it on): the album and artist names go to MusicBrainz (musicbrainz.org) to find the matching release, and the cover is then downloaded from the Cover Art Archive (coverartarchive.org). You choose the image before it is saved.

These services see your IP address and the search words needed to find a match.

  • Sending a crash log (optional): tapping "Send Crash Log" opens Android's share menu with the log file attached and an email address filled in. Nothing is sent unless you finish sending it in the app you choose. The log can include app activity, error details, and timestamps.
  • Sharing the crash log (optional): after a crash, or from the Crash log in Settings, Share crash log opens Android's share menu with the crash log and our email address filled in. Nothing is sent unless you finish sending it.
  • Your device's media controls: like other music players, Core Music shares the current track's title, artist, and artwork with Android's media controls, the lock screen, and any connected devices you use, such as Bluetooth or Android Auto.

Third parties

LRCLIB, MusicBrainz, and the Cover Art Archive, only when the matching switch is on and you start a lookup. Google Play Billing handles the purchase. Google Play services Block Store keeps a copy of the trial start date (see above).

Permissions

  • Music and video files / storage, and folders you pick: to find and play your files. Core Music can only reach the folders you choose with Android's folder picker.
  • Internet and network state: only for the optional lookups and Google Play Billing.
  • Notifications and foreground services: for playback controls and library scanning.
  • Audio settings and wake lock: for EQ and output, and to keep playback running with the screen off.
  • Battery optimization exemption (asked, optional): so playback and scans aren't stopped.
  • USB host (optional feature): for USB audio devices (DACs).
  • Google Play billing: for the unlock.

Purchases

Core Music offers a 14-day free trial, then a one-time $4.99 unlock through Google Play Billing. Google calls this kind of purchase a non-consumable one-time product: it is a single charge, it does not renew, and once bought it stays associated with the Google Account that bought it. Signing in on another device with the same Google Account is how the unlock is recognized again. Google's own terms allow a transfer between Google Accounts only when you own both accounts and Google has turned that transfer on for Play. (Payment, refund and card terms common to all apps are in section 2.)

10. Core Notes

Core Notes is a notes, calendar, and reminders app for Android: Encrypted Notes, Calendar, & Reminders. It supports text, checklist, photo, drawing, and audio notes, reminders, a calendar, a Clock with alarms, and widgets. No account is required. Everything Core Notes stores is encrypted on the device.

PDF attachments: PDFs you attach to a note are copied into Core Notes and stored encrypted on your device, the same way as your notes and images. They are never uploaded. When you export or share an attachment, Core Notes creates a decrypted copy and hands it to the app or location you choose; that copy is no longer protected by Core Notes.

Crash reports: if Core Notes crashes, it saves the error trace (no note text) to its private storage only, and can show it on a startup or lock screen with a Share button. Nothing is sent automatically. Share fills in our support address (gotocorepstx@gmail.com) and the subject Core Notes crash log; nothing is sent until you send it, and you can change or remove the address first.

No internet

Core Notes does not request Android's internet permission, so the app cannot connect to the internet. It has no crash-reporting SDK and does not fetch link previews.

What stays on your device

  • Your notes, checklists, labels, colors, reminders, calendar events (including events you import), reminder locations, reminder notes, and settings, in an encrypted database in the app's private storage.
  • Photos, drawings, and audio recordings you add, as encrypted files in the app's private storage.
  • A small preview image of each note's card, so Home can show it quickly. It is stored encrypted in the app's private storage and deleted with the note.
  • Crash reports from the last 5 crashes: the error, where in the app it happened, the app version, the device model, and the Android version. They contain no note text, and they stay on the device unless you share them.
  • A schedule of alarm times, kept outside the encrypted database so reminders can fire while the app is locked. It holds no note text, and no reminder titles unless you turn on note titles in reminders; if you do, those titles are also stored outside encryption.
  • Clock alarm times, repeat days, sounds, and the timer, kept outside the encrypted database so they ring while the app is locked. Alarm labels and your world clock cities are stored encrypted. The world clock uses the time zones built into Android, with no internet or location access.
  • Android backup is turned off for Core Notes.

Encryption

  • The database is encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256), including the search index, reminders, and calendar events.
  • Photos, drawings, and audio are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, including audio while it is recording.
  • The key is created on your device and protected by the Android keystore. With an app lock, it is also protected by your PIN or pattern, or a strong biometric. When Core Notes locks, the key is cleared from memory and the database is closed. The key is never sent anywhere, and Core cannot recover it.
  • Backups are encrypted, password-protected zip files. The backup password is separate from your app lock (at least 8 characters, with letters and numbers), and a recovery code can open the backup instead. Backups include notes, reminders, calendar events, and your Core Notes settings (theme, reminder defaults, widget, and list settings). App-lock settings, your PIN, pattern, and keys are never included. If both the password and the recovery code are lost, the backup cannot be opened, by us or anyone.
  • Widgets and reminder notifications hide content while the app is locked.
  • Not encrypted: notes you export as text, Markdown, PDF, image, or Word files, calendars you export as .ics files, anything you share to another app or copy to the clipboard, and the alarm schedule (including reminder titles, if you turn them on). Text you copy is cleared from the clipboard after 60 seconds if Clear copied text is on (the default).

Calendar import (optional, part of the unlock)

  • From your device's calendars: only when you tap Import calendar, Core Notes asks for permission to read your device's calendars. This reads the calendars Android already keeps on the device, such as those synced by Google, Samsung, or Exchange accounts. You pick the calendars and the date range. The events are copied once into Core Notes' encrypted database; you can re-import later by hand. Core Notes never writes to, changes, or deletes your device's calendars, and it never syncs in the background.
  • From an .ics file: opening or choosing an .ics file copies its events into Core Notes. The event title becomes the reminder name, the start and end become the date and time, the location goes in the Location field, and other details go into the reminder's notes as plain text. The original file is not changed or deleted.
  • Past events are imported as completed and never ring.

Calendar export

With the unlock, Calendar export creates a standard .ics file on your device. It goes only where you save or share it through Android's save or share screen. The .ics file is not encrypted.

PDF export

When you export a PDF, Core Notes reads any text it finds in your photos and drawings, on the device, and prints it as a caption under each picture. The text-recognition engine is built into the app, and nothing is uploaded.

Reminder sounds

Reminders can use the default sound, a system sound, or an audio file you pick. A file you pick stays where it is; Core Notes only keeps permission to play it. If it is moved or deleted, the default sound plays.

What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself. Backups and exports, including .ics calendar exports, are saved where you choose with Android's file picker. Sharing opens Android's share screen, and the receiving app's own policy then applies. Tapping a link opens your browser. Sharing a crash log (Crash report on the lock screen, Share crash log after a crash, or Settings › About › Crash log) opens Android's share screen with the log attached and our support address filled in, and nothing is sent unless you finish sending it.

Importing from other note apps

Core Notes checks whether Google Keep, Samsung Notes, Evernote, Simplenote, Standard Notes, or Joplin is installed, only so it can show import steps for those apps. It does not read their data. It imports only files you export and choose, or notes you share to it.

Third parties

None are contacted by Core Notes. Google Play Billing handles the optional unlock.

Permissions

  • Microphone and foreground service: only while you record an audio note, asked the first time you tap record.
  • Calendar (read only): optional, asked only when you import from your device's calendars. Core Notes never edits them.
  • Notifications: asked the first time you set a reminder.
  • Alarms & reminders (exact alarms): optional. Exact times are on by default, but Android honors them only after you allow Alarms & reminders for Core Notes. Without it, alarms, the timer, and reminders may ring up to about 10 minutes late.
  • Full-screen alerts (optional, in Settings): used only so alarms, the timer, reminders, and calendar events can fill the screen when they ring, even when your device is locked. If full-screen access is off, they show as a regular notification.
  • Display over other apps (SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW): optional and off by default. Core Notes asks for it only when you turn on Show alarms over other apps in Settings, which opens Android's own Display over other apps page. It's used only to open the alarm screen over other apps while your device is in use, and it reads no data.
  • Battery optimization exemption: asked only so alarms and reminders fire on time. Notes works without it.
  • Do Not Disturb access: asked only while Ring on silent for reminders is on (it is on by default, and you can turn it off in Settings so reminders follow silent mode). With access, reminders can ring during Do Not Disturb.
  • Foreground service: keeps an alarm or the timer ringing until you stop or snooze it, and a due reminder ringing until you tap Done or Snooze, or swipe it away.
  • Run at startup, wake lock, and vibration: to restore reminders and alarms after a restart or time change, and to ring and vibrate on time.
  • Biometric: optional fingerprint unlock. Android checks the fingerprint on the device; the app only receives a yes or no.
  • Photos: added with Android's Photo Picker, which needs no storage permission. Core Notes sees only the photos you pick, and re-saves them without location or other EXIF data.
  • Google Play billing: for the optional unlock.
  • Not requested: internet, location, contacts, camera, storage, or writing to your calendars.

Purchases

Core Notes is free. Encrypted notes, encrypted backups, the Quick Bar and Quick Box widgets, simple in-note reminders, and the Clock (alarms, timer, stopwatch, and world clock) are free. An optional $4.99 one-time unlock through Google Play Billing adds the calendar, repeating and advanced reminders, calendar and .ics import and export, and the Today and Month widgets. No subscription. The unlock is remembered on this device and works offline, and it never locks you out of notes you already have.

11. Core Scan

Core Scan is a document scanner for Android from Core Products & Services, LLC. It turns photos of pages into PDFs or JPGs on your device. No account is required.

No internet

Core Scan does not request Android's internet permission, so the app cannot connect to the internet. Your scans are not uploaded anywhere by Core Scan. Finding edges, cropping, filters, and text recognition all happen on your device. The first time you open Core Scan, and again after a reinstall, Android's Google Play Store app checks your Google Play purchase, so that start needs a connection. After that, Core Scan works offline. Core Scan has no crash-reporting SDK.

What stays on your device

  • Your saved scans. PDFs and JPGs are saved to Documents › Core Scanned Documents, where other apps on this device can open them. They are not encrypted, and they stay after you uninstall Core Scan unless you delete them.
  • Your settings, such as flash, auto edges, file size, name template, date format, icon, and text recognition languages.
  • If you reinstall Core Scan and choose the Core Scanned Documents folder so it can list your older scans, Android remembers that folder access for Core Scan. Core Scan only accepts that one folder.

  • The result of your Google Play purchase check, kept scrambled so it only works on this device.
  • Everything above except your saved scans is kept in the app's private storage. Android backup is turned off for Core Scan, so this data is not copied to your Google backup or moved to a new device.

Text recognition (optional, off by default)

Text recognition adds a hidden text layer so a saved PDF can be searched and its text selected. It is off until you turn it on. It runs entirely on your device using Tesseract, an open-source text recognition engine built into the app. All language files come with the app, so nothing is downloaded. When text recognition is on, Core Scan copies the files for the languages you choose into its private storage, and removes them when you turn a language or text recognition off. Those files hold none of your data. Core Scan does not keep a separate copy of the recognized text. It exists only inside the PDF you save.

What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself, plus one check: the first time you open Core Scan, and again after a reinstall, Android's Google Play Store app confirms your purchase with Google Play. Core Scan sends nothing itself, and Google's privacy policy applies to that check. When you share a scan, Android's share menu sends that file to the app you pick, and that app's own privacy policy then applies. Saved scans do not carry the location or camera details stored in an imported photo.

  • Send to Core Sign: opens the saved PDF directly in Core Sign on the same device, with read-only access, through Android. Nothing is uploaded. If Core Sign is not installed, Core Scan opens its Google Play page instead.
  • Opening a scan: a saved PDF opens in Core Sign when it is installed. If it isn't, you can get Core Sign on Google Play or open the PDF with another app on your device, and that app's own privacy policy then applies. A saved JPG opens in another app on your device, where that app's policy applies too. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Sharing the crash log (optional): after a crash, Core Scan asks once whether you want to share the crash log by email with gotocorepstx@gmail.com. You can also share it from Settings › About › Crash log. Nothing is sent automatically, and nothing is sent unless you choose to send it.

Third parties

Core Scan contacts no third parties itself. Android's Google Play Store app checks your purchase with Google Play. No third‑party ads. No analytics. No tracking.

Permissions

  • Camera: asked the first time you scan, and used only to take pictures of your pages while the scan screen is open.
  • Google Play license check: so Android's Google Play Store app can confirm your purchase.
  • Photos you import: chosen with Android's photo picker, which needs no storage permission. Core Scan sees only the photos you pick.
  • Folder access after a reinstall (optional): chosen by you with Android's folder picker.
  • Not requested: internet, storage, location, microphone, or contacts.

Purchases

Core Scan is a $4.99 one-time purchase on Google Play. No trial. No subscriptions. No in-app purchases.

12. Core Sign

Core Sign is a PDF reader with fill and sign tools for Android. Reading, saving, sharing, exporting, printing, and splitting PDFs are free. No account is required. Its vault is encrypted. The rest of the app is not.

No internet

Core Sign does not request Android's internet permission, so the app cannot connect to the internet. Your documents and signatures are not uploaded anywhere by Core Sign. All processing happens on the device.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings, your saved signature and initials, the documents you work on, and your folders (their names, colors, and order), kept in the app's private storage. Folders exist only in Core Sign; making, renaming, or moving between them doesn't change any file or folder on your device.
  • Copy or Link: each PDF you import is a Copy or a Link. For a Copy, Core Sign keeps its own copy, and your original is never changed. For a Link, Core Sign keeps a working copy for your edits and the file's address, and Android's file permission lets it reopen the original where it is. Photos, and files opened from another app, are always copies.
  • Your original files: Core Sign changes a linked original only if you choose Overwrite original, and deletes it only if you choose Delete from device (for a linked folder, that deletes the folder and everything in it). Each asks you to confirm first. Before overwriting, Core Sign keeps a backup of the original in its private storage and deletes it once the new version checks out; if the original can't be put back, the backup stays there.
  • Trash: documents you delete stay in Trash for 30 days, then are removed. A Link you remove from Core Sign is dropped right away, and Core Sign gives back its access to the original.
  • A crash log of the last 5 crashes, kept in the app's private storage: the error trace, the app version, the device model, and the Android version, with a text-layer report for the last page you opened (line positions and font names, never document text). Nothing is sent unless you share a log.
  • Android backup is turned off for Core Sign.

Vault

Your signatures, initials, and the documents you store in the Vault are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key stays in the Android keystore and is wrapped by a PIN or pattern you set, or a strong biometric if you turn it on. Your PIN or pattern always works too. Storing a document in the vault stores a copy; your original file is not deleted. When you open a vault document, the decrypted working copy is removed when you close it, and any copy left by a crash is cleared the next time the app starts. Documents you are working on outside the vault, and files you export, are not encrypted. Your PIN or pattern cannot be recovered. Repeated wrong attempts add a growing wait, up to 15 minutes.

What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself. When you share, export, or print, Android sends the file to the app or printer you pick, and that app's own policy then applies. If you link a file that a cloud storage app provides, Core Sign reads it, and writes or deletes it only when you choose to, through that app, which may sync the change under its own policy. Sharing a crash log opens Android's share menu with our email address filled in, and nothing is sent unless you finish sending it.

Third parties

None are contacted by Core Sign. Google Play Billing handles the optional unlock.

Permissions

No permissions to grant. Core Sign opens the PDFs, folders, and images you choose through Android's file picker. For a Link, Android lets Core Sign keep access to that file or folder until you remove it from Core Sign. The install-time permissions are Google Play billing, used for the unlock, and biometric, used only if you turn on biometric unlock for the vault.

Purchases

14. Core Weather

Core Weather is provided free of charge. It shows Dual °F/°C for a place entered by the user, or for the device location only if Detect is chosen. Detect is one-shot and opt-in. It is not continuous tracking, and it is not saved as a profile. Core Weather requires a connection for the forecast, place search, and alerts.

What stays on your device

  • Your settings (temperature units, sound, vibration, alert options, theme).
  • Your saved place (the text you typed, or the place that Detect location found).
  • The last forecast, with the rounded coordinates for your saved place and a list of alerts already shown, so widgets and notifications don't repeat themselves.
  • A crash log of the last 5 crashes, kept in the app's private storage: the error trace, the app version, the device model, and the Android version. It does not include your place or location. It is never sent automatically; sharing it opens Android's share menu with our email address filled in, and nothing is sent unless you finish sending it.
  • This data is kept in the app's private storage. Android backup is turned off for Core Weather, so this data is not copied to your Google backup.

What leaves your device

Core Weather needs the internet. All requests use HTTPS. Every request goes straight from your device to the service, so that service sees your IP address. Requests also carry a User-Agent label that names Core Weather.

  • Place lookup (when you type a place): the city or postal code you type is sent as you typed it. We cannot round a typed place name. Services used: Nominatim (OpenStreetMap), Zippopotam.us (5-digit U.S. ZIP codes), and the U.S. Census Geocoder (backup for ZIP codes only). Place search runs only when you search; it is never repeated in the background. By default, place search uses the public OpenStreetMap Nominatim server. In Settings you can enter a different place-search server; if you do, the place you type, or the rounded Detect point, goes to that server instead, under its own terms.
  • Detect location (optional): the app asks Android for your location one time, only when you tap Detect location. Before any network request, it rounds the coordinates to 1 decimal place (0.1°, roughly 11 km or 7 miles). The rounded point goes to Nominatim (OpenStreetMap) to find the place name. If no name is found, the place is saved as "Home" with the rounded coordinates. Then Detect location turns itself off. The app keeps the place it found, not a live location.
  • Forecasts: rounded coordinates go to MET Norway (the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, api.met.no). MET Norway logs the IP address and the requested coordinates on its servers in Oslo, Norway.
  • U.S. observations and severe alerts: for U.S. places, rounded coordinates also go to the U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov). Outside the U.S., NWS is not contacted.
  • Background refresh: while you have a saved place, Core Weather refreshes about every hour by default. You can change this to every 30 minutes, 1 hour, or 3 hours in Settings. Refreshes run only when you have a network connection. For U.S. places with alerts turned on, a separate alerts-only check runs about every 15 minutes; it does not run when alerts are off. Outside the U.S., background refresh runs only while a Core Weather widget is on your home screen; otherwise the forecast updates when you open the app. Opening the app fetches new data only if the last forecast is more than 10 minutes old. Each refresh sends the saved rounded coordinates to the forecast and alert services above, never your typed place name, and it never repeats a place search. Background refresh never turns on GPS. Widgets redraw from the saved forecast without a network request.
  • Links you tap: "More on this warning" and similar links open forecast.weather.gov or Windy.com in your browser, with rounded coordinates or your ZIP code in the address. That website's own policy applies.

Third parties

MET Norway, Nominatim / OpenStreetMap, Zippopotam.us, U.S. Census Geocoder, U.S. National Weather Service, plus forecast.weather.gov or Windy.com only when you tap a link. Core has no control over their logs.

Permissions

  • Internet and network state: to get forecasts and place data, and to refresh only when a connection is available.
  • Location (approximate and precise): optional. Used only when you tap Detect location. Typing a city or ZIP works without it.
  • Notifications and vibration: for weather updates and severe-weather warnings. You can turn these off in the app or in Android settings.
  • Run at startup, wake lock, and a short background task: to keep the scheduled refresh running after a restart.

Purchases

None. Core Weather is free and has no in-app purchases.
