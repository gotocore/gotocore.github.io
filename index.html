1. What privacy means

Privacy, as used in this policy, refers to the practices Core Products & Services, LLC does not undertake. Beyond the promises above, there are no analytics, no profiles, no cloud, no Core servers, and no social features. Core does not operate an anonymizing service. Encryption is used only where a section below says so: the Core Cycle database and backups, the Core Gallery vault, the Core Sign vault, everything Core Notes stores, and the FTP connections you save in Core Utilities. Core Weather, Core Music, Core Utilities, and Google Play purchases are the only things that use the internet, each described in its own section. Core Cycle has no internet access of its own; only Android's Google Play Store app, for purchases, uses it for Core Cycle. In Core Music, the optional Google Cast switch also uses your local Wi-Fi network, and if you turn it on, Google's own Cast library collects anonymous usage data for Google, not for Core (see section 18).

3. Rate or share pop-up

Core Gallery, Core Games, Core Notes, Core Scan, Core Sign, Core Weather, Core Workout, and Trio Live Wallpapers can show a "Rate or share" pop-up now and then. It appears no sooner than 90 days after you install the app, and only after you have opened the app on at least 5 different days. After that, it appears at most once every 90 days. Core Scan also waits until you have saved a scan, Core Sign until you have exported a document, and Core Workout until you have finished a workout. Core Games shows it only on its home screen, never during a game.

  • What stays on your device: to decide when to show the pop-up, the app keeps a few dates and counts (such as when it last showed the pop-up, the days you opened the app, and, where it applies, how many workouts you have finished) in its private storage. They are never sent anywhere and are not copied to your Google backup.
  • Rate on Google Play: opens the app's page in Android's Google Play Store app, or in your browser if the Play Store app can't open it. A rating or review you choose to leave there is handled by Google Play under Google's policies.
  • Share: opens Android's share menu with a short message and the app's Google Play link. Nothing is sent unless you pick an app and send it yourself, and that app's own policy then applies.
  • Not now: closes the pop-up, as do Back and tapping outside it.

The app itself sends nothing. The pop-up is built into each app and does not use Google Play's in-app review service.

4. Where you left off, and the app lock

Core Cycle, Core Gallery, Core Notes, Core Scan, Core Sign, and Core Utilities open where you left off. If you leave one of these apps and come back, you are on the same screen. If Android closed the app in the background to free memory, it opens again on the screen you were on. These apps do not send you back to their home screen on their own.

To do this, the app gives Android a short note about where you were. Android keeps that note in your device's memory, and Core never receives it. It holds only the place, never the contents: no note text, no entries, no scans, photos, or document contents, no search words, no PINs or keys, and nothing from a Vault. Android clears the note when you swipe the app away from your recent apps, force stop it, or restart your device, and the app then starts from its home screen. Android describes this here: https://developer.android.com/topic/libraries/architecture/saving-states

  • Core Cycle: which tab is open, the day you had open to edit, and the month and day selected in the calendar. Not what you logged for them.
  • Core Gallery: the tab, the albums, groups, and pages you had opened (by name and ID), the picture or video you had open (by ID), and how far you had scrolled. The Hidden view and the Vault are never kept, and neither are search words.
  • Core Notes: the tab, the folder, and the ID of the note you had open, never its title or text. After you unlock, the note opens again from the encrypted database.
  • Core Scan: the tab and the step you were on (camera, review, or crop, with the page number). The pages of an unfinished scan are kept separately, as described in section 20.
  • Core Sign: the screen and tab, the folder, and, for a document that is not in the Vault, which document it is, its page, and whether Reader mode was on. A Vault document is never named: Core Sign opens on the Vault screen, locked.
  • Core Utilities: which page or tool is open, not what you typed, measured, or picked. A tool opens in its starting state.

App lock. Core Cycle and Core Notes have an app lock with a PIN or pattern you set (and, if you turn it on, a strong biometric). Core Gallery's Lock on close uses your device's screen lock. Core Sign has the same timing for its Vault. In Settings you choose how long you can be away before the app asks again: Immediately, or 1, 5, 10, 15, or 60 minutes. The default is 10 minutes. Core Cycle and Core Notes also offer Once a day, which keeps the app unlocked until midnight.

  • The time is counted from when you leave the app. When it has passed, the lock screen covers the screen you were on, and after you unlock you are right where you left off.
  • Immediately asks again each time you come back. In Core Cycle and Core Notes, a return within 10 seconds does not ask again.
  • Whether the app is locked is kept only in memory, never on disk. An app that starts fresh always asks first.
  • Leaving always protects the most sensitive parts, whatever time you choose. Core Gallery's Vault locks whenever you leave it. In Core Sign, the decrypted working copy of a Vault document is removed whenever you leave the app. If you come back within the time you chose, the document opens again; if not, the Vault is locked.
  • Core Cycle and Core Notes only ask if you have set a lock. Without one, what you store is still encrypted on the device, but anyone holding your unlocked device can open it.

The recent-apps preview and screenshots. Core Cycle and Core Notes hide their screens from the recent-apps preview and from screenshots by default, and you can change this in each app's Settings.

15. Core Cycle

Core Cycle is a cycle tracking app for Android from Core Products & Services, LLC. It is a record-keeping and calculation tool: it stores what you enter and shows estimates worked out only from your own entries. It is not a medical device, it does not give medical advice, and it is not contraception. No account is required. Core Cycle is meant for ages 13 and over (see section 5).

Your health information

What you log in Core Cycle (period days, flow, symptoms, moods, notes, and the dates they belong to) is health information, and Core treats it with extra care. It stays on your device. Core Products & Services, LLC does not receive it, cannot see it, does not sell it or share it, and does not use it for ads, profiles, or analytics. The only ways your entries leave your device are the ones you start yourself: an encrypted backup, a CSV export, or something you share (see What leaves your device). Core Cycle does not use Health Connect, and it does not request location, contacts, camera, microphone, or device-calendar access. For what Core Cycle does not have (no account, no wearable import, no ads or tracking partners, no sharing with partners, no messages from Core), see section 7.

No internet

Core Cycle does not request Android's internet permission, so the app cannot connect to the internet. Your entries are not uploaded anywhere by Core Cycle. Core Cycle has no ads, no analytics, and no crash-reporting SDK. Android's Google Play Store app checks your Google Play purchase when you unlock Core Cycle and each time you come back to it. If that check can't run, Core Cycle keeps the last result it saved on your device, so you are not locked out.

What stays on your device

  • Your entries: for each day you log, whether it was a period day, the flow, symptoms (the built-in ones and any you add yourself), moods, and a note. They are kept in an encrypted database in the app's private storage.
  • Your settings for estimates and reminders: such as your usual cycle and period length, whether the optional fertile window is on (it is off by default), reminder choices, cycles you excluded, a pause on estimates, the first day of the week, and the order of your symptom buttons. They are kept in the same encrypted database.
  • Your trial and purchase record: the time your 45-day trial started, a few counters that stop a changed device clock from extending the trial, and the last result of your Google Play purchase check, in the encrypted database. The trial start time is also kept in one small separate file (see Android backup).
  • Where you were: which tab is open, the day you had open to edit, and the month and day selected in the calendar, as a short note that Android keeps on your device so Core Cycle can open where you left off (see section 4). It holds none of your entries.
  • Your keys: the data key that opens the database is protected by the Android keystore and by your PIN or pattern (see Encryption). Core cannot recover it.
  • Reminder schedule (not encrypted): if you turn reminders on, the times of the next reminders, whether the daily reminder is on and its time, and whether reminder details are on are kept in a small file outside the encrypted database, so reminders can fire while the app is locked. Reminder times follow your estimated dates.
  • App settings (not encrypted): such as the lock timing, whether screenshots are hidden, the color theme (Mauve, Dark, Mist, or Blush), whether the calendar shows a grid, whether the Home and Calendar drag bars are open, and how far you got in first-run set-up. They are kept in a small settings file because they must be read before the database opens. The launcher icon you choose is stored by Android.
  • First-run answers: the first time you set up Core Cycle, your answers (last period date, earlier periods, cycle and period length, and your fertile window and reminder choices) wait in that settings file until the encrypted database opens. Then they are moved into the database and deleted from the file. A backup or CSV file you pick during set-up is held only in memory until then.
  • A crash log: the last 5 crashes, kept in the app's private storage: the app version, the device maker and model, the Android version, the time, the error type, the first 200 characters of the error message, and the stack trace. Core Cycle does not add your entries to it. It stays on your device unless you share it (see What leaves your device).
  • How long it is kept: your entries stay on your device until you delete them, use Erase all data, or uninstall the app. Core does not keep a copy. Android backup keeps only the time your trial started, in your Google account; Google decides how long it keeps that backup. Three trial numbers (when it started, how much of it is used, and the time of the last update) are also kept in Google Play services on this device only, with no cloud copy. The crash log keeps the last 5 crashes. Files you save yourself (backups and CSV exports) stay until you delete them.

Erase all data (in Settings, confirmed twice) deletes your entries, settings, keys, and reminders from the device. It keeps the time your trial started, so erasing does not restart the trial. Encrypted backups and files you exported are not affected. Uninstalling Core Cycle or clearing its storage also deletes your entries, unless you saved an encrypted backup.

Encryption

  • Database: encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256), using a key created on your device.
  • Key protection: the data key is wrapped by a key in the Android keystore and, when you set an app lock, also by your PIN or pattern (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 600,000 iterations). You can also unlock with a strong biometric if you turn it on: Android checks it on the device, and Core never sees or stores your biometric data. Your PIN or pattern always works too. They cannot be recovered by Core or anyone else. If you forget them and have no backup, your entries cannot be opened. When Core Cycle locks, the key is cleared from memory and the database is closed. You choose when it locks again after you leave (see section 4).
  • Backups: encrypted with AES-256-GCM, using a key made from your backup password or recovery code (see Backups, import, and export).
  • Not encrypted: CSV files you export, anything you share to another app, the reminder schedule and app settings described above, and, if you turn on reminder details, the reminder text ("Period expected soon," "Log today," or "Estimated fertile window") that Android shows in the notification, including on the lock screen.
  • Screen protection: by default Core Cycle hides its screens from screenshots and from the recent-apps preview. You can turn this off in Settings.

Reminders

Reminders are optional. They use Android's inexact alarms, so they can arrive a few minutes late. By default a reminder shows only "Core Cycle" and "Reminder." If you choose the plain Tools launcher icon, reminders show that name and "Reminder" instead, and reminder details are not shown. Android may still show the name Core Cycle in your device's app settings and in the header of a reminder, and Core Cycle cannot change that. Reminders are set up again after a restart, a time or time zone change, or an app update.

Android backup

Android backup is turned on for Core Cycle, but only for one small file: the time your 45-day trial started. Your entries, settings, database, and keys are left out by explicit backup rules, for both cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. This is so reinstalling Core Cycle does not restart the trial. A restored start time can only shorten the trial, never extend it. Google runs that backup, not Core, and Core never receives the file. Google says Auto Backup keeps app data in your Google Drive and, on Android 9 and newer, end-to-end encrypts it using your device's PIN, pattern, or password. You can turn it off in Android's backup settings. To move your entries to a new device, use a Core Cycle encrypted backup. Core Cycle does not use Google Play services Block Store.

Backups, import, and export

  • Encrypted backup (.corecycle): saved where you choose with Android's file picker. It holds your logged days (dates, flow, symptoms, moods, notes), the symptoms you added, and your cycle and reminder settings. It does not hold your lock, keys, trial, or purchase result. It is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key made from a separate backup password you set (at least 8 characters, with letters and numbers) or from a one-time recovery code. If you lose both, nobody can open it. Core Cycle can remind you to make a backup (every 30 days by default). Core does not upload backups.
  • CSV export: a spreadsheet file of your days (date, period day, flow, symptoms, moods, note), saved where you choose. It is plain, unencrypted CSV text (UTF-8), so anyone who has the file can read it. Core Cycle shows a warning, and you confirm before the file is made. It is never created unless you start it, and Core Cycle does not send it anywhere.
  • Import: you can import a Core Cycle CSV file, a CSV file in the format published by drip, or a Core Cycle backup, from a file you choose, during first-run set-up or later in Settings. Core Cycle reads it on your device, does not change the file, and tells you how many rows it skipped. Core Cycle is not affiliated with drip.

What leaves your device

Only what you send yourself, plus purchase checks and the one small backed-up file described above. Core Cycle sends nothing itself.

  • Backups and exports: saved where you choose with Android's file picker. After that, where the file goes is up to you and the app or service that holds it.
  • Crash report (optional): after a crash, a Crash report button appears on the lock screen. It shows the last crash, and Share crash report opens Android's share menu with our email address filled in. Nothing is sent unless you finish sending it.
  • Privacy policy: tapping Privacy policy in Settings opens this page in your browser.
  • Purchase: Android's Google Play Store app handles the purchase and checks it with Google Play. Google's privacy policy applies to those checks.

Third parties

None are contacted by Core Cycle. Google Play Billing handles the optional unlock, and Google's Android backup, which Google runs and Core does not, can hold the trial start time.
